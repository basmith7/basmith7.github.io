What is real here, and what is not

Real: the forward pass, the softmax, the cross-entropy loss, the gradients, the weight updates. Every line redrawn during training is an actual number that changed.

  • This network holds a few thousand numbers. A model you would actually talk to holds hundreds of billions.
  • It sees one, two or three words of context. Real models see thousands and weigh them against each other with attention, which this has none of.
  • The loss never quite reaches zero, and that is correct. "brian was a senior solutions architect at" is followed by two different companies, so the honest answer is a spread, not a single word. Flip to one word of context and the spread gets much wider, because "at" alone could go anywhere.
  • The bars are the real output. The model never produces a word, it produces odds over every word, and something has to pick. Set to "always the top word" it is deterministic. Slide it toward "takes chances" and it samples from the odds instead, which is where the variety in a real model's answers comes from. Train it a little and the odds are spread; train it fully and they collapse toward certainty, because it has memorized the text rather than learned anything general.
  • The part that carries over exactly: once training is done, the sentences are gone. What is left is a pile of numbers that lean the right way, and the answer is rebuilt from scratch every time you ask.
